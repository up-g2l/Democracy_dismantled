    The ECI's U-turns

    A referee earns trust by being consistent. Again and again, the Election Commission has said one thing and later done another: refusing data and then releasing it, rejecting documents and then accepting them, defending changes and then withdrawing them. Many reversals came only after a court order, public pressure or objections from inside the Commission. Each case below shows what the Commission said first and what it did later.

      How to read this page

      Each case is one reversal. What it said first is the Commission's original position, statement or rule. What it did later is the change. Court covers Supreme Court and High Court orders that often forced the change. Investigation and Analysis are newsroom reporting that documented it.

      Changing course can be the right thing to do when a decision was wrong. The concern these reports raise is how often the Commission's first position was to withhold, refuse or deny, and how rarely it explained why it changed.
