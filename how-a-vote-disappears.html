    How a vote disappears

    Under first past the post, a seat is won by one vote more than the runner-up, and a government by a handful of seats. That is why small, targeted changes to a voter roll can matter more than millions of votes cast. Scroll to see how biased deletions, misused Forms 6 and 7, and a referee that does not answer add up, with the real cases behind each step.

    The constituency and the state shown in the animation are illustrations with made-up numbers. Every real figure is from a report linked on this site.

    Illustration · one constituency

    1

    Start with one constituency

    Picture 100 voters on a polling roll. Every name on it is a vote that can be cast. Every name taken off is a vote that cannot, whatever that person wanted.

    2

    First past the post

    India elects its Lok Sabha and state Assemblies by first past the post: whoever gets the most votes in a seat wins it, by one vote or by fifty thousand. Here Candidate A leads Candidate B by 51 to 49.

    The 49 votes for B elect no one. Only the margin decides the seat, so the margin is where manipulation pays. Nobody needs to change millions of votes; a few names in the right booths are enough.

    3

    Form 7: an objection in your name

    Form 7 lets a voter object to another name on the roll. In case after case, objections were filed in people's names without their knowledge, in bulk, and often against one community.

    • Aland, Karnataka: about 6,000 online deletion requests made by impersonating voters; a police investigation charge-sheeted a former BJP MLA and his son. Form 7 page
    • Bengaluru, 2026: FIRs over 5,698 Form 7s in Jayanagar and 136 forged forms in Malleshwaram. Godda, Jharkhand: a booth agent arrested over about 200 Form 7s against minority voters.
    • The software itself: the Election Commission's system generated Form 7s in officers' names with no reason recorded: 85,854 in Uttar Pradesh, and 5.38 lakh removals in West Bengal. Sources

    4

    Deletion: the vote is gone before polling day

    Flagged names are struck off at the roll stage, long before anyone reaches a booth. Remove four of A's supporters and B now leads 49 to 47. No ballot was touched, no machine was opened.

    • West Bengal: about 91 lakh names removed before the 2026 vote, roughly one voter in eight. West Bengal page
    • Uttar Pradesh: 2.89 crore names cut from the draft roll, nearly one in five. Uttar Pradesh page
    • Who: a study found Muslims were 95% of deletions in seven Nandigram lists; The Caravan found Bengal's exclusions rose with the TMC's lead and the Muslim population. Sources

    5

    Form 6: names added, details withheld

    Form 6 adds a voter. Additions decide seats too when they are not checked or published. Add three unexplained names and the gap widens: 52 to 47.

    • West Bengal: about 7 lakh voters added between the final roll and the close of nominations, without constituency details. Form 6 page
    • Uttar Pradesh: 16.18 lakh additions with no published applications; the state's election office says it was not required to publish them. Pilibhit: 10,470 changes to voters' details with no forms on file, and an FIR. Sources

    6

    A referee that does not answer

    The Election Commission is meant to catch all this. Instead, the record shows a referee that decides alone, explains little and corrects late. The deleted voters wait; the election does not.

    • Two of the three Election Commissioners objected on record 14 times in 10 months to decisions taken without them. Centralisation page
    • The Commission refused under RTI to name the software that flags voters, calling it a trade secret. Opacity page
    • In West Bengal, by August only about 2% of 38 lakh appeals had been decided, and about 91% of those put the voter back. The Supreme Court had said people with pending appeals could not vote. Accountability page

    7

    First past the post multiplies it

    Now zoom out to a state of 20 seats. Make the same kind of change only in the three closest seats, a few voters in each. Statewide, the vote share barely moves. The majority flips. A still wins more votes across the state, but B wins more seats and forms the government.

    This is the arithmetic of first past the post: seats are won at the margin, and governments are won in the close seats. Targeted changes to the roll in those seats can change who governs without most voters noticing anything.

    8

    It is not hypothetical

    • 105 of 207 seats the BJP won in West Bengal in 2026 had more SIR deletions than its winning margin, Scroll's analysis found. Source
    • 862 votes was the margin in one Bengal seat where, the TMC told the Supreme Court, more than 5,000 names had been deleted. Source
    • 131 + 36 Assembly and Lok Sabha seats in Uttar Pradesh were won by margins under 5%, while the state's roll now has 2 crore fewer names than in 2024, an analysis in The Wire noted. Source

    Deletions larger than a margin do not show how those voters would have voted, and the Election Commission says the revision removed dead, shifted and duplicate entries. But under first past the post, these are exactly the seats where a clean, fair roll decides who governs.

    9

    Every booth, every name

    A democracy run on first past the post rests on the integrity of each booth's roll. A few names here and there, in the right seats, are enough to change a result, and a result is enough to change a government. That is why every case on this site matters.
